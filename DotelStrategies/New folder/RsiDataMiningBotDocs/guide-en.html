How to Use RsiDataMiningBot

This guide explains how to load the strategy, compile it, run it in Strategy Analyzer, understand the parameters, and optimize it in a disciplined way.

Contents

1. What it is

An RSI research framework, not a single fixed setup

RsiDataMiningBot is designed to explore repeatable RSI behaviors without having to write a brand-new strategy for every hypothesis. Instead of one rigid logic path, it combines an RSI entry family, optional market context filters, protective orders, and exit rules.

The goal is to let you test many ideas in Strategy Analyzer while keeping the search space structured enough to remain useful.

2. Installation

How to load it into NinjaTrader 8

  1. Open NinjaTrader 8.
  2. Go to New > NinjaScript Editor.
  3. Make sure RsiDataMiningBot.cs is in your custom strategies folder.
  4. Press Compile.
  5. If compilation succeeds, the strategy will be available in Strategy Analyzer and on charts.
Expected path: Documents\NinjaTrader 8\bin\Custom\Strategies\DotelStrategies\New folder\RsiDataMiningBot.cs
3. First backtest

Baseline run to confirm the strategy behaves correctly

  1. Open New > Strategy Analyzer.
  2. Select Backtest.
  3. Choose RsiDataMiningBot.
  4. Pick a liquid instrument and a simple timeframe such as 5 or 15 minutes.
  5. Use a historical range that is short enough to iterate quickly but wide enough to be representative, such as 6 to 12 months.
  6. Run one single test with default settings before optimizing anything.
4. Parameters

How the strategy is organized

01. General Direction, quantity, trading hours, flatten outside the time window, max trades per session, and daily loss limit.
02. RSI Core Entry mode, RSI periods, thresholds, midline, lookbacks, and signal validation options.
03. Context Trend filters, ADX, ATR, and price action confirmation.
04. Exits SL and TP model, ATR multipliers or fixed ticks, and managed exits such as midline, opposite signal, or timeout.

That structure matters. The intended workflow is to optimize the RSI Core first, then add context, then refine exits.

5. RSI modes

What each entry family means

CrossBackFromExtreme

Mean-reversion logic. Long entries trigger when RSI was below the lower threshold and crosses back above it. Shorts mirror the process at the upper threshold.

ExtremeTouch

Entry on direct contact with an extreme level. This is the most aggressive mode and usually needs stronger filters.

CenterlineCross

Continuation logic through the RSI midline. This often behaves better in trending conditions than in pure mean reversion.

SignalLineCross

Uses a moving average of RSI as a signal line. Useful when you want smoother entries than raw threshold events.

RecoveryFromExtreme

Requires a recent extreme and then a recovery through the midline. More selective than a simple oversold or overbought touch.

BreakoutThroughExtreme

Momentum logic. Instead of fading extremes, it tries to exploit forceful movement through them.

SlopeReversalAfterExtreme

Requires a prior extreme and a meaningful RSI slope reversal. Useful when testing early acceleration after exhaustion.

6. Optimization workflow

A fast and disciplined testing sequence

  1. Step 1: lock instrument, timeframe, and session window.
  2. Step 2: optimize only EntrySignalMode, RsiPeriod, LowerThreshold, Midline, and ExtremeLookback.
  3. Step 3: freeze the best RSI family and add one context block at a time: trend or ADX or ATR.
  4. Step 4: optimize exits with limited freedom.
  5. Step 5: validate out of sample before trusting the result.
7. Avoiding overfitting

How to use flexibility without building curve-fit noise

  • Do not optimize every parameter at once.
  • Do not change instrument and timeframe while tuning the core logic.
  • Use sensible parameter ranges, such as RSI 5-21 and lower threshold 20-40.
  • Start with symmetric thresholds using UseSymmetricThresholds = true.
  • Require out-of-sample confirmation and preferably validation across a different market regime.
  • Look for neighborhoods of good settings, not one isolated peak.
8. Suggested presets

Three practical starting points

Basic Mean Reversion

CrossBackFromExtreme, RsiPeriod=5-9, LowerThreshold=25-35, TrendFilter=None, AdxFilter=Maximum.

Trend Pullback

RecoveryFromExtreme or SignalLineCross, TrendFilter=StackAndSlope, ProtectiveOrderMode=Atr.

RSI Momentum

BreakoutThroughExtreme or CenterlineCross, AdxFilter=Minimum, PriceActionFilter=BreakPreviousBar.

9. Reading results

What to inspect beyond net profit

  • Profit factor: more meaningful when supported by enough trades.
  • Drawdown: treat it as a primary metric, not a secondary one.
  • Trade count: small samples are fragile.
  • Period distribution: check whether the entire edge comes from one short exceptional segment.
  • Consistency: compare behavior across different market conditions.
10. Common mistakes

Typical issues and likely causes

No trades The filter combination may be too strict, or the time window does not match the active market.
Too many trades You are likely using ExtremeTouch without enough context filters or without spacing between entries.
Excellent but fragile results You probably optimized too many parameters at the same time or used a historical range that was too small.
Compiles but does not appear Check the namespace, errors in other strategies, and the actual file location.
